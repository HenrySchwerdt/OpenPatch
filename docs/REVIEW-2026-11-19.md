# Independent design review, 2026-11-19

Scope: does OpenPatch work as a pump, from first principles, with numbers. Method: read spec, STATUS, safety, mechanics (params, op_*.py, check.py, sim report, CNC package, REFILL/ASSEMBLY/parts), electronics/bench (INTERLOCK, pin-assignment, parts), firmware-rs core (limits, draw, reservoir, tmag5273), v0.1.0 architecture; recomputed key numbers with small scripts (power screw, seal/yoke/vent/socket numbers, magnet fields with magpylib, hall trigger distance). Nothing was modified except this file. Status words: OK, PROBLEM, UNVERIFIED (could not be shown from the repo or a reliable source). Severity: S1 pump does not work or is unsafe, S2 works badly / not trustworthy, S3 annoying or documentation.

Important state note: **the CAD, the CNC drawing package, `check.py`, the sim report and ASSEMBLY.md still describe the side-slot design.** Spec section 3b (push rod, closed reservoir) is a decision only; no geometry for it exists. Findings below about the push rod are therefore design-level findings on a decision, not on a model.

## 1. Executive summary: top 10, ranked

Ranking = "pump would not work" first, then "unsafe / untrustworthy", then "annoying".

1. **S1. The plunger seal friction budget is not closed, and nothing in the repo shows it can be.** Modelled kinetic seal friction 9.6 N (range 3-20 N), breakaway 14 N, against a drive that delivers 4.2 N at 105 mA, about 6.4 N at the current firmware setting (160 mA) and 7.3 N at the rated 181 mA (my recomputation reproduces the sim). Sim rows 1.3, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8 FAIL; the sim report still uses 105 mA while the firmware already runs 160 mA, so the headline numbers are stale in both directions. The spec's "about 9.5 N" (section 4) is wrong (ignores collar friction, current limit, 0.64 efficiency twice). Consequence: with the drawn seal (2 rings, 0.13 mm squeeze per side) the pump probably stalls on every dose; a stall is read as occlusion. Fix: one thin lip (contact width <= 0.4 mm), squeeze <= 0.07 mm/side, silicone grease film, ball/pin thrust end (+60 %), 160-181 mA after a thermal check, and measure the real seal on a pull-test coupon before anything else is ordered.
2. **S1. The "central push rod on a yoke" decision (spec 3b) inherits a new problem: a 11.3 mm yaw lever on a 4 mm carriage.** The screw axis is at y 22.5, the bore axis at y 11.2. A yoke carrying the rod loads the carriage with a yaw moment M = F x 11.3 mm (6 N gives 68 N*mm). The carriage is guided by a 0.9 mm rail in a 1.2 mm groove, 4 mm long: guide reaction about 17 N, drag at mu 0.3 about 5 N, which is as large as the available thrust. The old tongue had a 3.5 mm lever (1.6 N drag), so this is 3.2x worse. The nut float (0.3 mm) and the 25 mm M1.6 rod also take side load. Fix: do not offset the pusher from the screw. Put the screw on the bore axis (motor beside, gear pair to the screw), nut fixed in/behind the plunger or a driven nut with the screw fixed to the plunger (Insulet style), plunger stadium shape gives anti-rotation for free. Section 5.
3. **S1. End-cap-to-reservoir joint has no seal.** Spigot d1.98 into a d2.0 H7 port (+0.012/0): a 0.020-0.032 mm diametral clearance fit in a printed SLA part (+-0.05..0.1 mm), plus a flat resin face against a flat PP face, no O-ring, no gasket, no adhesive. This is the same class of mistake as the slot: a joint on the wetted path with no engineered seal. It will weep, and under draw vacuum (20-30 kPa) it admits air. Fix: face O-ring or 0.3 mm silicone gasket in a groove, or a tapered (Luer-slip style) port; ask the CNC shop for a counterbore for an O-ring (the CNC part is not yet ordered, so this is free now).
4. **S1/S2. Air path in draw mode is uncontrolled.** Candidate air entries: the septum (disc d4.0 punched, in a d4.2 bore: no radial squeeze, 3.2 mm deep bore for a 2.0 mm disc, no defined axial preload), the cap face (item 3), the needle hub (PTFE d2.0 in a d2.0 seat, zero interference), the duckbill pocket (sim 5.5). Dead volume 2.2 U path + 2.2 U air layer, single central port, no way to purge the dry-end-up bubble except holding the pod port-up. Nothing in firmware can tell water from air in a draw (angle and current only), so a pod that "drew 100 U" of air books 100 U as the reservoir level. Fix: elastomer seals everywhere (O-ring/gasket), vacuum hold test as a mandatory POST/assembly test, a refill procedure with the port up, and a gravimetric fill check before doses are trusted.
5. **S1 (bench) / S2. Lost motion at every reversal is larger than the firmware compensates.** Carriage-to-plunger socket play: socket 0.8 mm wide, shim 0.5 mm: 0.3 mm of free travel = 2.2 U, plus nut play 0.03-0.08 mm (0.2-0.6 U). Firmware take-up (sim recommendation, 152 steps) is 80 um = 0.6 U. After a draw the first prime/dose loses up to 2-3 U. With a push rod the socket disappears only if the rod-to-plunger link is play-free (needs a captured ball or a press-fit head). Fix: zero-play link, take-up of at least 700 steps (0.3 mm / 0.53 um per step = 567 steps + nut play) or detect take-up by the current signature.
6. **S2. Basal accuracy is set by stick-slip, not by resolution.** One full step moves the plunger 0.53 um (3.95 mU). To break a 10-14 N seal away the train must wind up 10 N / 220 N/mm = 45 um = about 85 steps = 0.34 U before anything moves, then releases as a jump. At 0.05 U (12.7 steps) basal pulses the delivered dose is essentially "nothing, nothing, 0.3 U burst". Sim row 2.8: 80 % error at 0.05 U. Fix: friction by design (item 1), stiffer train (1.2 mm shim or steel pin), pulse trains delivered as dithered bursts instead of single quanta; the spec's "0.05 U granularity" is a quantisation claim, not an accuracy claim.
7. **S2. Hardware over-current/current-sense scaling mismatch.** The board has a 0.333 ohm shunt (R31/R39/R47 in parallel) and INA180A1: 6.67 mV/mA (`pin-assignment.md:27`, `INTERLOCK.md`). `firmware-rs/openpatch-bench/src/config.rs:139` still says "1.0 ohm: 20 mV per mA". Firmware current readings are therefore low by 3x: a real stall of 267 mA reads 89 mA and never reaches `OCCLUSION_CURRENT_MA` (213), so current-based occlusion and draw-stall detection are dead on this hardware. Fix: one constant, bench-calibrated with a known load, plus a unit test that ties the number to the netlist.
8. **S2. The TMAG5273 angle decode may be reading the wrong plane.** Layout: the spindle magnet (axis along pod X) puts a rotating field in the pod Y-Z plane (layout.json says so). Firmware enables channels X and Y only and sets `ANGLE_EN = X-Y` (`tmag5273.rs:36-39`). If package X is along pod X (the layout says the 2.9 mm body length is along pod X), the X channel sees only the constant axial component and the "angle" is not a rotation angle. UNVERIFIED because the package axis definition is in the TI datasheet figure I could not read here; layout.json is also self-contradictory ("pin row along pod Y" vs "lead span along pod Y"). Also the A1 +-40 mT range: my calculation gives 34-41 mT at 2.05 mm on axis for N52 (Br 1.45) and 28-34 mT for Br 1.2; layout.json says "about 12 mT" and 35.3 mT in two fields. Marginal at +-40 mT: use the +-80 mT range bit or move the sensor 0.5 mm away. Fix: one bench test with a hand-turned rod before any firmware trust.
9. **S2. Length growth and the "smallest pump" goal.** Push-rod closed reservoir adds about one stroke: OP-70 52.4, OP-100 60.4, OP-200 87.2 mm (spec 3b says 47 to 60 for OP-100; consistent). The OP-200 would be longer than every commercial patch pump (DASH 52 mm) although still smaller in volume (87.2 x 32 x 8.4 = 23.4 cm3 vs 29.4). It is physics, not a flaw: the only coupling that avoids the extra length is a flexible element or a telescoping screw (section 4). Decide per size; OP-200 should not use a straight rod.
10. **S2/S3. Manufacturability of the end cap and several docs/CAD disagreements.** d0.7 mm channels with a 9.9 mm run and three 90 degree turns inside a printed resin part cannot be flushed or inspected (uncured resin, dust); many 0.40-0.55 mm walls in MJF PA12 (documented exceptions) are below normal vendor minimums (UNVERIFIED against JLC3DP rules); FH12 FFC connector is 2.0 mm high against a 1.0 mm CAD envelope (does not fit under the lid, 0.4 mm interference); the CNC drawing package still has the slot; the housing "30 min at 1 m" claim in `02-safety.md` contradicts the open FFC slot and porous PA12.

Honest overall verdict: the electronics, firmware discipline and tooling are far ahead of the mechanical core. The mechanical core (seal, coupling, joints) has not been shown to work once, and the project keeps stacking verified-looking layers on it. **Do not order the CNC reservoir, the end cap, or the carriage until a seal-friction/leak coupon (item 1, 3, 4) has been built and measured.** A 20-EUR coupon (printed bore or PP tube, cast seal, force gauge, syringe, balance) answers items 1, 3, 4, 5 in one evening.

## 4. Comparison with commercial patch pumps

What the literature shows (patent documents, not teardowns; I could not find a reliable Medtrum internal teardown):

* **Insulet** (Omnipod family): US 12,329,928 (continuation family of US 10,420,883) claims a plunger in a reservoir with a **leadscrew extending from the plunger and a nut threaded onto it, turned by a drive wheel through a clutch**; the plunger is keyed in the reservoir so the screw cannot rotate. The nut rotates, the screw (fixed to the plunger) translates, so push and pull are one rigid connection and there is no offset lever. Source: [US 12,329,928](https://patents.justia.com/patent/12329928).
* **Telescoping screws**: [US 12,329,927](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12329927) (multiple telescoping screws, innermost keyed to the plunger; notes that efficiency falls as nested screws increase), [US 9,539,384](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9539384) (telescoping piston drive coaxial with the plunger), [US 11,980,739](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11980739) (double-acting telescoping screw outside the reservoir). Telescoping cuts the extra length from one stroke to about half or a third at the price of lower efficiency (a bad trade for this motor, see item 1).
* **Flexible tape drive**: a patent hit describes a motor turning a geared shaft that advances or retracts a flexible tape against the plunger ([patent listing](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8517991) in the search results; claims not read). This is the commercial form of the "spring-steel band in a U guide" study in spec 3b. It allows a curved reservoir/motor beside it without a length penalty.
* **Gearhead scale**: steel Faulhaber 06/1 gearheads are trade-press-rated about 25 mNm continuous ([im-tek listing](https://www.im-tek.com/faulhaber-06-1-641-planetary-gearhead-amp.html)); the drive here needs about 3 mNm, so the gearhead is not the limit if the NFP part is comparable (UNVERIFIED).
* Source on the TMAG5273: the angle plane and package axes are in the TI datasheet ([SLYS045B](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLYS045B/GUID-D92D9F03-88C2-4A54-B10F-AA9D63CEA714)); I could not read the figure and rely on the repo's notes.

Shared by the commercial designs and absent here:
1. **Rigid, axial plunger link with no offset**: the pusher sits on the reservoir axis and the anti-rotation comes from the plunger shape, not from a carriage rail.
2. **A closed reservoir**: commercial reservoirs are a closed vessel with a sealed rod or a keyed plunger; the dry side is vented through a membrane.
3. **Reservoir as a pre-filled or fill-and-purge cartridge with an engineered seal at every joint** (O-ring or molded gasket), not flat-to-flat faces.
4. **Short stroke per size by changing the cross-section**, not only the length. At 8.4 mm height the cross-section is fixed, so OP-200 is long by nature; the commercial answer is a flexible/curved element or accepting a larger pod.

What OpenPatch should copy: the Insulet-style driven nut with the screw fixed to a keyed plunger (or equivalently a screw on the bore axis with the nut captured in the plunger), a bubble-aware reservoir geometry (port on the top flat), a vent membrane, and O-ring seals. Keep: the geared stepper, the modular dock, the water-only discipline, and the very good hardware interlock/diagnostic structure.

## 5. Yoke detail: why the yoke does not work and what to do instead

Numbers (F = push force, arm = lateral distance between screw axis and push line, guide length 4.0 mm, mu 0.3):

| F | Old tongue (3.5 mm) | Yoke to bore axis (11.3 mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 N | M 14 N*mm, guide drag 1.1 N | M 45 N*mm, drag 3.4 N |
| 6 N | 21 N*mm, 1.6 N | 68 N*mm, 5.1 N |
| 10 N | 35 N*mm, 2.6 N | 113 N*mm, 8.5 N |

Options, in order of preference:
A. **Screw on the bore axis, plunger carries the nut** (nut captured in a plunger rear recess; screw rotates, driven by the motor beside via the existing 16:20 gears). No carriage, no rail, no yoke, anti-rotation by the stadium plunger, push and pull rigid. Length cost: one stroke (OP-100 about 60 mm), same as the yoke. The plunger needs a rear recess about one stroke deep (a hollow plunger tube guided in the bore): check the tube stiffness and the seal-to-tube alignment.
B. Driven nut, screw fixed to the plunger (Insulet): the gear sits on the nut, the screw's tail projects behind the nut by one stroke at the full state. Same length, simpler plunger.
C. For OP-200: flexible element or telescoping screw, or accept 87 mm.
