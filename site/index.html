How it works

Small parts.
Careful design.

A drive you can verify, an interlock that does not trust software, and a link to the apps people already use.

A drive that checks itself.

A 6 mm stepper turns a fine spindle that sits beside the reservoir. A magnet and Hall sensor count what actually moved. One step delivers 0.026 U, and the firmware stops if steps and rotation disagree.

Two keys for the motor.

The driver only gets power when the processor and an independent watchdog both agree. If the firmware hangs, the hardware cuts the motor.

Speaks Trio. Natively.

A dedicated Trio pump kit, or a pod that speaks the Omnipod DASH protocol. The link exists to exercise the protocol against a pod on the bench.

Charges on a pad.

A flat Qi coil means no port to seal. Delivery pauses while the pod is on the charger.

Refilled with a syringe.

A small silicone septum on the side takes the fill, water on the bench.

A docking study.

The pocket targets an Accu-Chek Insight Flex cannula head, as a purely mechanical interface exercise.

Concept: two pods, one at a time.

Two identical pods trade places. One is docked and delivering while the other charges and is refilled. A magnet and Hall switch tell a pod whether it is docked, and an explicit release-and-activate handover makes sure only one can ever deliver. A design concept, studied on the bench.

Safety

Designed to fail safe.
Still never for people.

The safety engineering is there so that bench tests mean something, not to make wearing it acceptable. It is not.

Independent hardware limit

A second circuit, not the firmware, decides whether the motor may run. A hung processor means a stopped pump.

Every dose is verified

A sensor on the spindle measures real movement. A mismatch beyond 10 % stops delivery and sounds an alarm.

Hard ceilings, fail-quiet never

Maximum bolus, basal rate and hourly total are fixed in firmware. After any reset the pod suspends and alarms rather than carrying on silently.

Build it

One new version
every month.

Order-ready files for a bench prototype. Run it on water, into a beaker or a balance. Never on a person.

  1. 1

    Order the boards

    Upload the Gerber, BOM and placement files to JLCPCB, or PCBWay. They fabricate and assemble every component.

  2. 2

    Print the shell

    Send the STL files to JLC3DP or PCBWay for nylon or resin printing. Tolerances are designed for it.

  3. 3

    Press it together

    Battery and motor contact the board through spring contacts that the lid presses down. Tweezers, a small screwdriver and a UV lamp are all you need.

  4. 4

    Flash and pair

    Load the firmware, pair it with a test setup, and run the bench test plan with water on a precision balance.

Questions.

Can I wear it or use it with insulin?

No. Never. It is a test project, not a medical device, and must not be worn, connected to a person, or used with insulin. Use approved devices.

What is the idea?

A design question: how flat can a refillable, rechargeable patch pump be if every part is open, orderable and replaceable? The pod is built to be opened, changed and rebuilt.

Which cannula head does the pocket target?

An Accu-Chek Insight Flex head. The exact snap geometry is not public, so the pocket is a placeholder until measured; a measuring sheet is in the repository.

Does it speak Omnipod 5?

No. Omnipod 5 pairing relies on signed transcripts and a key manager that cannot be emulated. The DASH-compatible mode is enough to exercise the protocol.

What can be assembled without soldering?

Everything. Boards arrive assembled. Battery and motor press onto spring contacts, and the shell snaps and screws together.

Where is it at today?

Mechanical Rev A for three sizes is final, with an upload-ready fabrication package. A netlist and BOM, a tested firmware core and a Trio kit exist. The first boards are not yet ordered.
